## 4. Overall research-mentor assessment

Candidate 68 identifies a potentially useful scientific seam: uncertainty about the physical and informational state at the boundary between concurrent planning and commitment. Its strongest conceptual choice is to keep no-start delay inside PIE-D while treating processing and observation uncertainty as uncertainty over the state from which an exact PIE proposal may be committed. The exclusions of wrong-edge motion, alternative-path repair, same-tick speed variation, and full-horizon belief replanning give the proposed object a recognizable boundary.

That conceptual distinction is not enough to validate the frozen specification. The current theorem ladder mixes four state categories that must be separated, has no linearization semantics for an envelope that evolves while planning and preparation continue, and gives a MOVE acceptance rule that conflicts with ordinary collision-free handoffs in a singleton MAPF state. The distributed commit and fencing claims assume the unique authority property they are supposed to establish. The compactness claim also places quantified `MAY_OCCUPY` and `MUST_OCCUPY` sets into the input without showing that they can be maintained or queried without solving the original joint-cut problem.

These are bearing objections, not requests for cosmetic clarification. Under Candidate 68's own sequential-review rule, any one of them requires new bytes under a new candidate number. Therefore the frozen candidate is REJECTED. The broader journal route remains CONDITIONAL rather than permanently rejected because the core boundary-state question may survive in a formally rebuilt successor.

## 5. Bearing objections

Each objection in this section independently bears on safety, theorem validity, identity preservation, or the claimed scientific contribution. None may be waived by a later reviewer.

### BO-68R1-01: The commit-boundary envelope has no decision-time linearization invariant

Candidate 68 defines the envelope as cuts consistent with accepted evidence, but physical execution can advance throughout OBSERVE, envelope construction, PREPARE, grant collection, and COMMIT. It never identifies the instant or protocol event denoted by the "true boundary cut." Event completion is monotone, while current occupancy is not: a sound occupancy report can be historical by the time a grant is used. A static envelope that contained the state when evidence was collected need not contain the state when commitment or departure occurs.

This gap blocks T68-2 and T68-4. Safety under unbounded processing time cannot follow from a snapshot-level containment statement unless the envelope is updated inductively or resource preparation freezes every relevant transition before the decision uses it.

Closure requirement: a successor must define the physical/protocol transition system, evidence-time semantics, the commit linearization point, and an inductive invariant showing containment at every safety-relevant transition from observation through physical start. This can remain timing-symbolic; no timing bound is needed or requested.

### BO-68R1-02: Physical occupancy, exclusion reservation, protocol authority, and transaction obligation are conflated

The candidate uses "owner" for both the authority that issues grants and the agent whose occupancy must be protected. It also orders destination acquisition before physical arrival, while defining `MAY_OCCUPY` as physical occupancy and requiring a unique physical phase. A prepared reservation, an exclusive exclusion claim, physical presence, authority over an epoch, and an origin-release or destination-acquire obligation are different state types with different transition rules.

This type collapse makes resource witnesses ambiguous. In particular, it is unclear whether destination acquisition means reservation or physical occupancy, whether an authority appears in occupancy sets, and what current owner/fence state proves about physical absence. T68-COMP and T68-5 therefore lack a well-typed state space.

Closure requirement: a successor must use separate predicates and lifecycles for physical occupancy, exclusion reservation, grant authority, transaction state, and release obligations. Every event must state which typed predicate it creates or discharges.

### BO-68R1-03: The MOVE rule conflicts with singleton inactive identity on legal PIE handoffs

The proposed rule rejects `MOVE(i,u,v)` whenever destination `V(v)` has a conflicting possible occupant. Consider a certified singleton state in which agent `j` occupies `v`, PIE proposes `j: v -> w`, and PIE simultaneously proposes `i: u -> v`. This following move can be a valid discrete MAPF batch when its transitions are ordered consistently and it is not an edge swap. A rotation provides the same issue. Candidate 68's literal destination test rejects `i` because `j` is in `MAY_OCCUPY(V(v))` at the boundary.

The batch-atomicity sentence does not define an atomic resource handoff that resolves this contradiction. Consequently, the wrapper cannot currently guarantee T68-6 exact action-vector identity for every certified singleton baseline state. It may also reject a broad, routine class of PIE actions, undermining the claimed nontriviality and throughput.

Closure requirement: a successor must either formalize safe atomic vacate/acquire dependencies and prove exact preservation of every legal singleton PIE batch, or explicitly narrow the baseline semantics and withdraw the unrestricted inactive-identity claim. This issue directly triggers Candidate 68's own falsification condition concerning singleton identity.

### BO-68R1-04: Per-action MOVE-to-WAIT substitution lacks batch closure

Changing one proposed MOVE to WAIT changes the occupancy against which every other proposed action must be checked. For example, if `i: u -> v` is suppressed and `i` waits at `u`, an unchanged `j: w -> u` may now collide with `i`. SH-DYNAMIC checks already committed incoming transactions, but the text does not explicitly include incoming actions in the same proposed batch, nor does it specify a deterministic fixed-point or closure procedure after substitutions.

The phrase "the proposed batch has no conflict" appears as a condition on preserving a MOVE, but no emitted-batch construction proves that checks are repeated against the final WAIT/MOVE vector. If safe holding is unavailable, "containment" is invoked without a state transition, resource footprint, or safety postcondition.

Closure requirement: define a global batch refinement operator in which WAIT occupies its holding resource, substitutions propagate through all incoming and resource-dependent actions, and the final vector is checked as a whole. Prove termination, exact-MOVE-or-WAIT output, and collision freedom. Either formalize containment as part of the transition system or remove it from T68-4's claimed output class.

### BO-68R1-05: C1 resource ownership and fencing assume the split-brain property they must prove

The protocol states that each resource has one current authority and that ownership changes advance a monotonic epoch. Under partition, restart, and component merge, those statements are not self-enforcing. Two isolated components can each believe they hold a newer valid epoch unless authority changes are controlled by a quorum, a non-duplicable physical fence, or another explicitly trusted serialization mechanism. Saying that new commits stop "without the current authority" assumes that every component can already distinguish the current authority.

Likewise, collecting PREPARE grants and emitting one COMMIT does not by itself specify durable decision recording, atomic visibility, recovery after commit-authority failure, or executor deduplication after reboot. Message idempotence keys prevent duplicate processing only while the receiver retains a trustworthy ledger. A boot/incarnation field does not specify how old commits are fenced from a new incarnation.

T68-5 and T68-8 are therefore circular under the stated partition and merge model.

Closure requirement: state the process, channel, crash/restart, storage, and trust failure model; define the authority-reconfiguration mechanism; identify durable records and executor acceptance rules; and prove that no two live authorities or executable commits can exist for the same protected epoch/resource history. If this is delegated to a trusted physical interlock or consensus service, make that an explicit assumption and narrow the protocol contribution accordingly.

### BO-68R1-06: Release and safe-holding semantics do not yet support a physical collision theorem

The event order releases the origin after certified departure and the edge after arrival. "Departure begins" does not necessarily mean the robot's swept volume has cleared the origin, and arrival at the destination does not necessarily mean the swept volume has cleared the edge. Releasing on those weaker events can authorize a follower into still-occupied physical space.

SH-BRAKE additionally invokes physical phase, stopping ability, and a protected resource boundary without defining an abstract dynamics model or a sound certificate interface. SH-DYNAMIC can also cease to hold between its check and commitment unless continued holding is itself reserved atomically. These are not merely implementation details because the candidate claims physical collision safety rather than only symbolic vertex safety.

Closure requirement: introduce explicit origin-cleared and edge-cleared events tied to a declared resource-footprint abstraction; define the assumptions under which a braking/holding certificate is sound; and include the hold entitlement in the atomic resource decision. Without physical validation, conclusions must be scoped to the declared abstract model rather than real-world robot safety.

### BO-68R1-07: T68-COMP moves the joint-state problem into undefined projection oracles

`MAY_OCCUPY(r)` and `MUST_OCCUPY(r)` are defined by existential and universal quantification over all admissible cuts in `E`. Treating these sets as already available shows that a final safety query can consume projections; it does not show that the projections can be computed, updated, or certified without enumerating cuts or solving an equivalent global feasibility problem.

The proposed resource-separable event-DAG class is not defined, no projection algorithm is given, no representation or update complexity is stated, and the exact role of cross-resource correlations in action transitions is unresolved. The intended proof step that local witnesses correspond to admissible joint cuts is tautological only if exact `MAY_OCCUPY` has already been computed; it is false for arbitrary combinations of independently derived local witnesses.

This blocks the central journal claim because compact storage alone is not the promised reduction.

Closure requirement: define the separable class syntactically, give a sound general-case algorithm and an exact class-specific algorithm, specify their input/output representation and complexity, prove projection realizability, and demonstrate that the class includes nontrivial corridor, tree, and intersection structures without silently eliminating useful handoffs. If projection queries remain equivalent to joint-cut enumeration on all useful instances, the stated route fails by Candidate 68's own criterion.

### BO-68R1-08: The conditional-progress theorem is not valid under the listed assumptions

Eventual communication, eventual terminal physical outcomes, fair per-resource service, valid holding, and repeated feasibility do not alone eliminate distributed prepare deadlock, repeated abort cycles, authority churn, or starvation across multi-resource transactions. The alternative outcome "receives a newer explicit reason for rejection" can also be repeated forever and is not progress.

Closure requirement: define a continuously enabled transaction, add the required stable-epoch and durable-recovery assumptions, specify deadlock prevention or resolution and transaction-level fairness, and state a measurable terminal liveness property. Otherwise T68-9 must be weakened to safety-only nonblocking diagnostics rather than eventual transaction progress.

## 6. Novelty, scope, and semantic separation from PIE-D

The candidate does not merely rename PIE-D delay. E68-D is explicitly retained as the no-start delay in which the robot remains at its origin. E68-P and E68-O instead concern what boundary state is knowable and safe to use after physical execution and information delivery evolve during planning. This is a credible problem distinction, but it is a conceptual assessment rather than an externally verified novelty finding. T68-0 still needs a formal projection or bisimulation showing reduction to PIE-D when observation and boundary state are exact, plus a pair of executions with the same delay behavior but different admissible information states to prove non-equivalence.

### 6.2 Scope risk

The candidate begins as a compact boundary-information wrapper but also claims a partition-tolerant, fenced, multi-resource transaction protocol and physical stopping semantics. That second layer can dominate the research burden. A successor should declare whether the protocol is:

- a trusted substrate expressed through a narrow interface, leaving the event-envelope theorem as the contribution; or
- a co-contribution with its own failure model, state machine, safety proof, and evidence plan.

Leaving it between those roles makes both novelty comparison and theorem responsibility unclear.

The candidate itself lists route-killing overlap tests against PIE, PIE-D, fixed-plan dependency execution, ADG-style execution, belief-space MAPF, timing-uncertainty planning, robust MAPE, and protocol-observation discrepancy work. ## 7. Assumptions and impossibility boundary

T68-1 has the right proof shape: construct two observation-indistinguishable worlds, one free and one occupied, and show that any policy authorizing contested entry collides in one world. The candidate correctly distinguishes a safety impossibility from an engineering inconvenience and correctly permits loss of liveness under unbounded communication delay.

The current minimal-trust statement nevertheless needs sharper typing. Cryptographic integrity can establish message origin and immutability but not physical truth. A sound positive release certificate requires a declared trusted computing base, a physical event semantics, replay protection, and a rule preventing later reacquisition from being hidden. The alternative physical interlock must likewise state which conflict relation it enforces.

Path conformance and complete resource expressibility are strong but legitimate modeling assumptions if advertised prominently. For realistic physical intersections, the graph/resource construction must encode swept-volume conflicts, including conflicts between geometrically crossing motions that do not share a graph endpoint. Otherwise the claim is collision freedom only in the abstract resource model.

## 8. Event-envelope and theorem-ladder assessment

The use of occurrence indices and an event DAG is materially better than an unconstrained Cartesian product. It preserves path revisits and some causal correlation, and the lower/upper ideal concept is a plausible starting abstraction. The theorem ladder also appropriately labels its statements as proof targets.

However, an interval of ideals plus local occupancy projections is not yet an operational abstract domain. A successor needs:

- a concretization function from compact states to physical/protocol traces;
- transfer functions for every report and protocol event;
- proof that transfer preserves over-approximation under reordering and omission;
- consistency handling for empty envelopes and contradictory trusted evidence;
- a decision-time invariant as required by BO-68R1-01;
- separate safety and exactness theorems, with complexity and nontriviality stated explicitly.

T68-3 must precede downstream claims that consume exact `MAY_OCCUPY` and `MUST_OCCUPY`. T68-5 or an explicit trusted-protocol assumption must precede T68-2 and T68-4. T68-6 must be tested against ordinary following moves and rotations before inactive identity is claimed. The present theorem numbering does not yet reflect those logical dependencies.

## 9. MOVE-or-WAIT refinement assessment

The restriction to exact PIE MOVE or safe WAIT is clear and scientifically useful because it prevents a safety wrapper from quietly becoming a replanner. Logging a witness for every changed action is also a strong accountability contract.

The refinement currently needs a batch-level semantics, not a per-agent checklist. The final decision must be calculated over the post-refinement vector, including WAIT occupancy, already in-progress transactions, atomically handed-off resources, and all cascaded suppressions. The point-of-no-return branch also needs a formal distinction between "no newly emitted action" and the continuation of a previously committed physical transition.

Until BO-68R1-03 and BO-68R1-04 are closed, T68-4, T68-6, and T68-7 cannot all be true at once.

## 10. C1 resource ownership and commit-protocol assessment

The proposed use of transaction identifiers, proposal digests, epochs, fencing, idempotent message handling, and pre-departure abort is directionally appropriate. It correctly rejects wall-clock lease expiry as a sole safety release and distinguishes ACK from physical completion.

The missing element is the source of serialization. One commit authority per transaction does not imply one authority per resource after reconfiguration, and independent PREPARE grants do not themselves produce a durable atomic decision. Safety can be recovered in a successor through a clearly assumed serialization substrate or a complete protocol model, but the present C1 contract is not a proof-capable protocol. BO-68R1-05 is therefore bearing.

## 12. Counterexamples and degradation

The existing counterexample suite is broad and relevant. It covers false completion, optimistic boundary prediction, duplicate execution after a lost ACK, clock-skew leases, partitions, mid-edge failure, unsafe WAIT, nonretreatable corridors, starvation, stale epochs, Cartesian over-conservatism, and the no-trusted-absence impossibility. The degradation table also correctly separates safety from conditional progress.

The successor's mandatory symbolic trace suite should add the following witnesses because they expose current theorem gaps:

- Singleton following move: an occupied destination is vacated by another exact PIE MOVE in the same legal batch.
- Rotation handoff: every destination is occupied at the boundary but the exact PIE cycle is collision-free under the baseline semantics.
- WAIT cascade: suppressing one MOVE leaves its origin occupied while another unchanged MOVE enters it.
- Observation-to-commit drift: the true cut advances after a sound report but before grants are used.
- Split-brain reconfiguration: partitioned components independently advance or accept authority histories.
- Commit-authority crash: failure occurs after durable decision at one participant but before all parties or the executor learn it.
- Executor reboot: an old COMMIT is replayed after volatile deduplication state is lost.
- Premature clearance: departure begins while the origin swept volume remains occupied, or arrival occurs while the edge swept volume remains occupied.

These additions do not replace proofs. They are minimum falsification witnesses for the corrected semantics.

## 13. Simulation-only evidence viability

Simulation-only evidence is conditionally viable for a journal route only if the paper is framed as a formal algorithm/protocol result over an explicit abstract execution model. Finite simulation cannot prove universal collision safety, envelope containment, protocol fencing, or the impossibility theorem. Those claims require mathematical proofs, and protocol interleavings benefit from an independently checkable formal transition model or model-checking artifact.

Simulation can validly provide complementary evidence for:

- inactive byte identity;
- triggered exact-MOVE-or-WAIT accountability;
- throughput and completion consequences;
- conservative rejection from lost correlation;
- starvation and tail-wait behavior;
- deterministic replay of every counterexample and degradation trace;
- agreement between the compact checker and an exhaustive oracle on tractable audit instances.

Simulation alone cannot validate real sensor truth, physical interlock behavior, swept-volume clearance, or braking certificates. Without physical validation, language such as "physical collision safety" must be explicitly qualified as safety in the declared simulator/resource abstraction. This limitation must be reflected in title, abstract, theorem assumptions, and conclusion.
